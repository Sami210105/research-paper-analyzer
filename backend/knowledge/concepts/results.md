Results are the empirical outcomes of the study.

What strong results look like:
- Compared against baselines, not reported in isolation
- Statistical significance reported (p-values, confidence intervals)
- Both quantitative metrics and qualitative analysis
- Honest about cases where the method underperformed

What weak results look like:
- Only absolute numbers with no baseline
- Cherry-picked examples
- No error bars or variance reported
- Evaluated on a single dataset only
